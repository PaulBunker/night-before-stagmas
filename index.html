A Poem for the Wicker Man Stag Do

The Night Before Stagmas

in honour of Hugh Jones, groom & guest of the day

'Twas the night before Stagmas, and all through the land,

The lads were assembling, with pints close at hand.

The costumes were ready, the secrets well-kept,

While Hugh Jones lay dreaming, quite peacefully slept.

For tomorrow was coming, a day of renown,

When the groom would be crowned the strangest man in town.

No ordinary stag do, no tame pub-bound affair,

But a pagan procession with menace to spare.

The whispers had started, the rumours had spread,

Of strange island rituals and things best left unsaid.

Of bonfires and maypoles, of dancing and song,

And a Wicker Man towering, twelve feet tall and strong.

ᚺ ᚢ ᚷ ᚺ

The lads were all plotting, their grins broad and wide,

With innocent faces and evil inside.

They'd lured Hugh to the island with promises sweet,

Of a weekend of drinking, good food and a treat.

"There's nothing to worry about, Hugh," they all cried,

"Just a wholesome wee gathering, a jolly lads' ride!"

But the sheep stood in circles, the crows gathered near,

And someone had purchased a suspicious-looking spear.

The Morris men practised their steps in the rain,

While someone rehearsed how to summon the slain.

The landlord poured pints with a sinister smile,

And the locals kept chanting, "He'll be ours in a while."

For Hugh Jones, dear Hugh, was the guest of the day,

The groom and the sacrifice — well, so people say.

His stag crown was waiting, his fate had been sealed,

And tomorrow the island's dark secrets revealed.

ᛃ ᛟ ᚾ ᛖ ᛋ

There'd be drinking and singing, debauchery and cheer,

Strange rites that grow stranger with each passing beer.

The best man would preach of the old ways of yore,

While the lads sacrificed dignity, socks and much more.

And if Hugh should ask, "What's that smoke in the sky?"

They'd say, "Just a barbecue, mate — don't ask why."

If he heard distant chanting, he'd be told not to fret:

"It's the locals' folk choir. You haven't met them yet."

Then the drums would beat louder, the night would turn red,

And the Wicker Man's shadow would loom overhead.

The lads would raise tankards and bellow his name,

As Hugh Jones marched onward towards glorious fame.

ᛋ ᛏ ᚨ ᚷ

So sleep well, brave Hugh, while the moon's shining bright,

For tomorrow brings madness from morning till night.

May your liver be mighty, your courage be vast,

And your hangover merciful when the day has passed.

For the vows may be sacred, the marriage divine,

But tonight you're the groom of a much stranger kind.

And when dawn breaks tomorrow, let all understand:

Hugh Jones shall be Stagmas King of the Wicker Man!

So raise up your glasses, let no man depart,

For tomorrow we honour our groom with dark art.

May the ale flow like rivers, may the old gods decree—

Happy Stagmas to Hugh!
